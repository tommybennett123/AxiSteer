The problem

Canes make users work for every step

Sustained postures

The upper body holds awkward positions for long periods.

Wrist and back pain

Constant tapping and bending add up to real strain.

Obstacle challenges

Navigating around hazards takes effort and concentration.

Ground-level only

Traditional canes only mark obstacles on the ground.

The solution

Haptic, directional, vibrational

AxiSteer senses the environment for you and tells your hand which way to go.

1

3-tier feedback

Graded vibration tells the user how close and how serious an obstacle is.

2

Sense naturally

The environment is felt through the handle, with no app and no screen.

3

Low centre of mass

Weight sits low on a single rubber wheel, keeping the cane light in the hand.

4

Localised processing

All computing and AI run on the device, in line with UK and EU GDPR.

✓

The result

Less bending and tapping, lower strain, more confidence.

Prototype

Internal layout: LiDAR, Arduino, two servos, a 100/24 mm wheel and the battery, all in a housing of roughly 100 × 150 mm. A servo attaches to the lower beam with a swivel ball joint.

Regulatory and legal considerations

Classification

The intended-purpose statement sets the legal route. AxiSteer aims to be a Class I medical device under UK MDR 2002: it treats nothing, is not invasive, and makes no injury-prevention claims.

Components

Every component must meet its own compliance specification.

Data and AI

All processing happens on the device, in compliance with UK and EU GDPR.
